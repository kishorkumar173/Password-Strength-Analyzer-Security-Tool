Diceware Multi-Word Passphrase

xkcd #936 Standard

Passphrases chain multiple dictionary words chosen uniformly at random. They maximize length and memory retention while frustrating automated dictionary and brute-force tools.

Why Passphrases?
A 4-word random passphrase like correct-horse-battery-staple provides ≈50+ bits of entropy with 28 characters of length, which is exponentially harder to crack offline than a 8-character string like P@ssw0rd!

NIST SP 800-63B Alignment

NIST Digital Identity Guidelines explicitly advise against mandatory composition rules (mixing characters) and arbitrary periodic resets (e.g. 90 days), as they induce users into predictable pattern choices. Instead, NIST emphasizes minimum length (≥8, recommended 12+), testing against compromised/dictionary lists, and supporting spaces for passphrases.

The Storage Pipeline:

Plaintext Password → Random Salt (16B) → Slow Key Stretching → Stored Hash String

Salt: Mitigates Rainbow Tables so identical passwords hash uniquely.

Key Stretching: Deliberately consumes CPU/memory cycles to bottle-neck GPU cracking rigs.

The 10 Golden Rules of Modern Password Security

IAM & Cyber Awareness
01

Unique Credentials Everywhere

Never reuse passwords. If one vendor suffers a breach, automated bots immediately test your email and password across hundreds of high-value services (Credential Stuffing).

02

Prioritize Length Over Composition

Length is the single most mathematically potent barrier to exhaustive search. Aim for 14-16+ characters or multi-word passphrases.

03

Eliminate Personal Identifiers

Do not include your name, birth year, pet's name, or employer. Attackers scrape public social media profiles (OSINT) to generate custom attack dictionaries.

04

Ban Common Keyboard Walks

Sequences like qwerty, asdf, and 1qaz are hard-coded into every password cracking mutation engine.

05

Avoid Formulaic Substitutions

Changing password to P@ssw0rd123! provides false security. Attackers use rule engines that exhaustively test leetspeak and year suffixes in milliseconds.

06

Use an Audited Password Manager

Tools like Bitwarden or 1Password generate, store, and auto-fill 20+ character random secrets, completely removing the burden of human memorization.

07

Enforce Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)

Even an astronomically strong password cannot defend against phishing or session hijacking. App-based MFA (TOTP) or FIDO2 hardware keys are essential.

08

Never Transmit Over Insecure Channels

Never share passwords in plaintext via email, SMS, or chat platforms. Store credentials only in encrypted vaults.

09

Stay Alert to Phishing & Deception

Verify URLs and SSL certificates before entering secrets. Attackers routinely craft convincing cloned login portals.

10

Rotate Upon Suspected Compromise

Modern standards (NIST) advise against arbitrary 90-day rotations, but immediate rotation is mandatory whenever a breach, malware infection, or unexpected notification occurs.
